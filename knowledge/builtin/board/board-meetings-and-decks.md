# Board meetings and board decks

## Purpose of board meetings
Board meetings exist to fulfill governance duties, review performance, advise on strategy and make decisions reserved to the board. The most valuable meetings spend most of the time on a few strategic questions rather than on presenting results that could be read in advance.

## Cadence and preparation
Many companies hold board meetings quarterly, with additional meetings for major decisions. Send the board pack several days in advance. Brief board members individually on sensitive or complex topics before the meeting to avoid surprises in the room.

## A typical board deck
Executive summary with the key messages and decisions requested. Performance against plan, including financial results, key metrics and variances with explanations. Cash position and runway. Progress on strategic priorities. Two or three discussion topics framed as questions. Items requiring approval, such as budgets, option grants or major contracts. Risks and mitigations. A detailed appendix for reference.

## Writing for the board
Lead with the conclusion. Be candid about bad news early and with a plan; boards lose trust when problems are hidden. Use consistent metric definitions and formats over time so trends are visible. Keep the main deck concise and move detail to the appendix.

## Running the meeting
The chair, together with the chief executive, sets the agenda and keeps time. Allocate most time to discussion. Hold an executive session without management when appropriate. Record decisions and action items in minutes reviewed by counsel or the company secretary where required.

## After the meeting
Follow up promptly on action items, share relevant outcomes with the leadership team, and collect feedback from board members on how meetings can be more useful.
