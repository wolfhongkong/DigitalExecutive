# Corporate governance basics

## Scope of this note
This is general orientation, not legal advice. Directors' duties and governance requirements depend on the jurisdiction, the company form and whether the company is listed.

## The role of the board
The board oversees the company on behalf of shareholders and, depending on jurisdiction, other stakeholders. Typical responsibilities are approving strategy, appointing, evaluating and compensating the chief executive, overseeing financial reporting and risk, approving major transactions and ensuring compliance. Management runs the company day to day; the board should not manage operations.

## Directors' duties
Common legal duties include a duty of care (acting on an informed basis with reasonable diligence) and a duty of loyalty (acting in the interest of the company and avoiding or disclosing conflicts of interest). Directors should document their deliberations, ask questions and seek expert advice for significant decisions.

## Board composition
Effective boards combine relevant expertise, independence and diversity of perspective. Independent directors without material ties to management or major shareholders provide objective oversight. Separating the chair and chief executive roles, or appointing a lead independent director, strengthens oversight.

## Committees
Larger and listed companies typically delegate work to committees: an audit committee for financial reporting, internal controls and external auditors; a compensation or remuneration committee for executive pay; and a nominating and governance committee for board composition and succession. Committees prepare recommendations; the full board remains responsible.

## Two-tier and one-tier systems
Some countries, for example Germany, use a two-tier system with a management board and a separate supervisory board, which may include employee representatives. Others use a single board with executive and non-executive directors.

## Good governance practices
Clear delegation of authority, annual board evaluations, director onboarding, conflict-of-interest registers, documented minutes and succession planning for the chief executive and other key roles.
