# Investor relations and investor updates

## Why investor communication matters
Regular, honest communication builds trust with investors, makes future fundraising easier and turns investors into a source of help through introductions, hiring and advice. For listed companies, investor relations are also governed by disclosure regulation.

## Regular investor updates
Private companies commonly send monthly or quarterly updates. A useful structure: headline summary, key metrics with comparison to plan and prior period, highlights, lowlights and what is being done about them, cash and runway, hiring, and specific requests for help. Consistency of format and metrics allows investors to see trends.

## Communicating bad news
Share bad news early, with context and a plan. Investors are rarely surprised by problems but often lose confidence when they learn about them late or second-hand. Distinguish facts from interpretation and say what you need from investors.

## Listed companies
Public companies must follow disclosure rules, which typically require prompt disclosure of inside information and equal treatment of investors. Earnings communication includes results releases, calls and guidance. Use a disclosure policy, designated spokespeople and quiet periods before results. Legal and compliance review is essential.

## Managing the investor base
Understand each investor's goals, fund timeline and follow-on capacity. Keep the capitalization table accurate. Build relationships with prospective investors before raising, by sharing occasional updates.

## Confidentiality
Investor updates can be forwarded. Avoid including highly sensitive details such as specific customer contract terms or personnel issues unless shared under explicit confidentiality obligations.
