# Fundraising and valuation basics

## Matching capital to the business
Equity financing, from angels, venture capital or strategic investors, suits businesses with high growth potential and uncertain cash flows. Debt suits businesses with predictable cash flows or assets. Non-dilutive options include grants, customer prepayments and revenue-based financing. Venture capital expects outcomes large enough to return its fund, which may not match every company's goals.

## Stages and expectations
Pre-seed and seed rounds typically fund product and early market validation. Series A usually requires evidence of product-market fit and a repeatable way to acquire customers. Later rounds fund scaling and depend increasingly on efficiency metrics such as growth rate, gross margin, net revenue retention and burn multiple (net burn divided by net new annual recurring revenue).

## Valuation and dilution
Pre-money valuation is the value before new money; post-money valuation equals pre-money plus the new investment. The new investors' ownership is the investment divided by post-money valuation. Option pool increases are often required before the round and dilute existing shareholders, so compare offers on an effective pre-money basis.

## Key term sheet terms
Economic terms include valuation, liquidation preference (often 1x non-participating as a common standard), anti-dilution protection (broad-based weighted average is more founder-friendly than full ratchet) and option pool size. Control terms include board composition, protective provisions requiring investor consent and information rights. A high valuation with aggressive terms can be worse than a lower valuation with clean terms.

## Running a process
Prepare a concise narrative, a data room with financials, key metrics, cap table and material contracts, and a target list of investors that fit the stage and sector. Run a time-boxed process to create momentum, keep the business performing during the raise, and keep a disciplined record of each investor conversation.

## Professional advice
Securities and tax rules differ by jurisdiction. Use qualified legal and financial advisers for term sheets, securities filings and option plans.
