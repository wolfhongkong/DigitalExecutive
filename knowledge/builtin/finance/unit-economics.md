# Unit economics

## What unit economics measure
Unit economics describe the revenue and costs associated with one unit of the business model, such as a customer, an order or a subscription. They show whether growth creates or destroys value. A business with negative unit economics loses more money the faster it grows unless the economics improve with scale.

## Contribution margin
Contribution margin is revenue minus variable costs directly attributable to the unit, such as cost of goods sold, payment fees, shipping, hosting and variable support. It shows how much each unit contributes toward fixed costs and profit. Gross margin uses accounting cost of goods sold; contribution margin is usually more useful for decisions because it includes all variable costs.

## Customer acquisition cost
Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is total sales and marketing spend in a period divided by the number of new customers acquired in that period. Use fully loaded CAC, including salaries, tools and agency fees, not just advertising. Track CAC by channel and segment, because blended averages can hide unprofitable channels.

## Customer lifetime value
Customer lifetime value (LTV) estimates the total contribution margin a customer generates over the relationship. A simple approximation for subscriptions is average monthly revenue per customer multiplied by contribution margin percentage, divided by monthly churn rate. LTV is sensitive to churn assumptions; use observed cohort data where available and be cautious when the business is young.

## LTV to CAC and payback
A common benchmark for subscription businesses is an LTV to CAC ratio of about three or higher. CAC payback period, the months of contribution margin needed to recover CAC, is often more practical: under about 12 months is considered strong for many small and mid-market subscription businesses, while enterprise businesses tolerate longer paybacks. Benchmarks vary by industry and stage.

## Improving unit economics
Levers include raising prices or average order value, reducing variable costs, shifting spend to efficient channels, improving conversion rates, reducing churn and increasing expansion revenue. Analyze by cohort so that improvements are measured on comparable groups of customers.
