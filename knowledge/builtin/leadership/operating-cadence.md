# Operating cadence and effective meetings

## What an operating cadence is
An operating cadence is the recurring rhythm of meetings, reviews and reports through which a leadership team sets direction, monitors execution and makes decisions. A good cadence makes priorities visible, surfaces problems early and reduces ad hoc escalation.

## A typical cadence
Weekly: a short leadership meeting focused on key metrics, risks and decisions needing the group. Monthly: a business review of financial and operational performance against plan, by function or business unit. Quarterly: a review of objectives and key results, resource allocation and the next quarter's priorities. Annually: strategy refresh, budget and organizational planning. Board meetings usually follow the quarterly rhythm.

## Designing a productive meeting
Every recurring meeting should have a clear purpose (inform, discuss or decide), an owner, the right attendees and a written agenda. Send pre-reads in advance and assume they have been read. Spend meeting time on discussion and decisions rather than status updates, which can be shared in writing.

## Metrics and dashboards
Use a small set of leading and lagging indicators per function, with targets and owners. Show trends, not just the latest value. Agree on definitions so the team does not argue about what a number means. A metric without an owner and a target is rarely acted on.

## Decisions and follow-through
End each meeting by confirming decisions, owners and deadlines. Keep a decision log and an action list that is reviewed at the next meeting. Unresolved items should be escalated deliberately, not carried forward indefinitely.

## Communicating the cadence
Share the outcomes of leadership meetings with the wider organization on a predictable schedule. Consistent communication builds trust and reduces the informal rumor network that fills gaps.
