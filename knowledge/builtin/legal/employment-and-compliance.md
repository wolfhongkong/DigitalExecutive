# Employment law and compliance basics

## Scope of this note
This is general orientation, not legal advice. Employment, data protection and regulatory requirements vary significantly by country and sometimes by region. Involve qualified HR and legal professionals for specific situations.

## Employment relationships
Distinguish employees from independent contractors; misclassification can create liability for taxes, social contributions and employee rights. Written employment contracts should cover role, compensation, working time, notice periods, confidentiality and intellectual property assignment. Some countries require specific written terms or works council involvement.

## Termination and restrictive covenants
Dismissal rules range from at-will employment in parts of the United States to strong protection against unfair dismissal in much of Europe, with consultation requirements for collective redundancies. Non-compete clauses are restricted or unenforceable in some jurisdictions and may require compensation in others. Follow documented, consistent processes and seek advice before dismissals.

## Anti-discrimination and workplace conduct
Most jurisdictions prohibit discrimination based on protected characteristics in hiring, pay, promotion and dismissal. Maintain policies against harassment, a confidential reporting channel and a process for prompt, fair investigation. Whistleblower protection laws may require specific reporting channels.

## Data protection
Laws such as the EU General Data Protection Regulation require a lawful basis for processing personal data, transparency, data minimization, security, respect for individual rights and, in many cases, records of processing activities. Personal data breaches may need to be reported to authorities within strict deadlines.

## Building a compliance program
Identify the regulations that apply to the business, assign owners, write proportionate policies, train employees, monitor and audit compliance, and provide a way to raise concerns without retaliation. Tone from the top matters: employees follow what leaders do more than what policies say.
