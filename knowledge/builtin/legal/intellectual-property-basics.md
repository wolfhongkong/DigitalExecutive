# Intellectual property basics

## Scope of this note
This is general orientation for business leaders, not legal advice. Intellectual property rights are territorial and their requirements differ by country.

## Main types of intellectual property
Patents protect new, inventive and industrially applicable inventions for a limited period, generally up to 20 years, in exchange for public disclosure. Copyright protects original works such as software code, text and designs automatically on creation, without registration in most countries. Trademarks protect names, logos and other signs that identify the source of goods or services and can last indefinitely if used and renewed. Trade secrets protect valuable confidential information for as long as reasonable measures keep it secret.

## Ownership of employee and contractor work
In many jurisdictions, work created by employees in the course of employment belongs to the employer, but the rules vary and do not automatically cover contractors. Use written agreements that assign intellectual property and include confidentiality obligations for employees, contractors and consultants. Missing assignments are a frequent problem in fundraising and acquisition due diligence.

## Protecting trade secrets
Restrict access on a need-to-know basis, mark confidential documents, use non-disclosure agreements with partners, manage access when employees leave and keep records of what is considered secret. Without reasonable protection measures, legal remedies may be unavailable.

## Freedom to operate
Before launching a product in a new market, consider whether it might infringe existing patents or trademarks. A freedom-to-operate review by counsel is advisable for significant investments, especially in patent-intensive industries.

## Open-source software
Open-source licenses impose obligations. Permissive licenses such as MIT and Apache 2.0 mainly require attribution. Copyleft licenses such as the GPL may require distributing source code of derivative works under the same license in certain circumstances. Maintain an inventory of open-source components and their licenses.

## Trademarks in practice
Search for conflicting marks before adopting a brand, register key marks in the markets where you operate or plan to, and use them consistently.
