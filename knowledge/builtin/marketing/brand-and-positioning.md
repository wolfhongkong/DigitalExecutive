# Brand and positioning

## Positioning defined
Positioning is the deliberate choice of how an offering should be perceived by a target customer relative to alternatives. A clear position answers: for which customers, in which category, delivering which key benefit, unlike which alternatives, because of which proof points.

## Building a positioning statement
Start from the customer's alternatives, which may include doing nothing or using spreadsheets, not only direct competitors. Identify the capabilities that are unique, translate them into value that customers care about, and define the customers who care most about that value. Choose a market category that helps customers understand the offering quickly.

## Messaging
Messaging turns positioning into words for different audiences and channels. A messaging framework typically includes a core value proposition, three to four supporting pillars with proof points, and tailored messages for each persona. Test messaging with customers and measure response rather than relying on internal preference.

## Brand
A brand is the set of associations people hold about a company: what it does, how it behaves and how it makes them feel. Brand identity includes name, visual identity, tone of voice and values. A consistent brand experience across product, service and communication builds trust and can support premium pricing.

## Brand architecture
Companies with several offerings choose between a branded house, where one master brand covers all offerings, a house of brands with independent brands, or a hybrid with endorsed sub-brands. A branded house is efficient for awareness; separate brands suit very different audiences or risk profiles.

## Measuring brand
Track awareness, consideration, preference and net promoter score in the target segment, share of voice in relevant media, branded search volume, and pricing power. Brand building is long-term; evaluate it over quarters and years rather than weeks.
