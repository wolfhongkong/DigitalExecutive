# Corporate communications and public relations

## Role of communications
Corporate communications manage how the organization communicates with employees, customers, investors, media, regulators and the public. Consistent, credible communication protects reputation and supports strategy.

## Message discipline
Define a small number of key messages and supporting facts. Align spokespeople, provide them with media training and prepared answers to difficult questions, and route external statements through an approval process proportionate to the risk.

## Media relations
Build relationships with journalists who cover your sector before you need them. Offer genuine news, data or expert perspective rather than promotional content. Respect embargoes, respond promptly, and never mislead; assume anything said may be published.

## Internal communications
Employees should hear important news from leadership before or at the same time as the public. Explain the reasons behind decisions, what changes for employees and where to ask questions. Regular channels, such as all-hands meetings, written updates and manager cascades, build trust.

## Crisis communications
Prepare before a crisis: identify likely scenarios, form a crisis team with clear roles, prepare holding statements and contact lists, and rehearse. During a crisis, respond quickly with verified facts, show concern for affected people, say what is being done and when the next update will come, and correct misinformation. Do not speculate. Coordinate with legal counsel, but avoid statements so defensive that they damage trust.

## Measuring communications
Track share of voice, sentiment, message pull-through in coverage, reach among target audiences, employee understanding of key messages and reputation surveys. Media coverage volume alone says little about impact.
