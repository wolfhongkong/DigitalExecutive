# Scaling operations

## What changes with scale
Practices that work for a small team, such as informal communication and founders approving everything, break as headcount, customers and geographies grow. Scaling requires deliberate investment in structure, systems and management capacity before the old way fails visibly.

## Organizational design
Design the organization around the work: functional structures build expertise, while product, customer or regional structures improve speed and accountability for outcomes. Keep spans of control reasonable, commonly about five to ten direct reports depending on the work, and define clear ownership for every important outcome.

## Systems and tooling
Replace spreadsheets and manual workarounds in core processes, such as finance, customer relationship management, HR and support, with systems of record before data quality deteriorates. Integrate systems so that data flows without re-entry. Choose tools that will fit for the next two to three years of growth, not the next ten.

## Capacity planning
Forecast demand and translate it into capacity requirements for people, infrastructure and suppliers, with lead times. Hiring plans should account for recruiting time and ramp-up to full productivity. Monitor utilization; sustained utilization above about 85 percent usually means long queues and little room for unexpected work.

## Quality and reliability
Growth amplifies defects. Define quality standards and service levels, monitor them, and build feedback loops from customers and front-line staff. Document critical procedures and test business continuity plans.

## Keeping speed while adding structure
Push decisions to the lowest level with the right information, use clear decision rights and guardrails instead of approvals, and regularly remove processes that no longer add value. Structure should make good work easier, not slower.
