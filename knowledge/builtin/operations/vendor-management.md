# Vendor and supplier management

## Strategic sourcing
Segment purchases by spend and supply risk. Strategic items with high spend and high risk deserve close partnerships; leverage items with high spend and low risk can be competitively tendered; bottleneck items with low spend but high risk need security of supply; routine items should be simplified and automated. This prioritizes procurement effort.

## Choosing a vendor or supplier
Define requirements and evaluation criteria before contacting vendors. Use a request for information to understand the market and a request for proposal for formal comparison. Evaluate total cost of ownership, not just price: implementation, integration, switching costs, support and exit costs. Check references, financial stability and security or compliance certifications where relevant.

## Negotiation
Know your alternatives and walk-away point, understand the vendor's incentives such as quarter-end targets, and negotiate on value: term length, volume commitments, payment terms, service levels and exit support, not only unit price. Multi-year commitments should include price protection and performance remedies.

## Contracts and service levels
Service level agreements should specify measurable targets, measurement methods, reporting and remedies such as service credits. Include data protection, security, audit rights, business continuity and exit provisions for critical vendors.

## Ongoing management
Assign an owner for each important vendor relationship. Hold regular business reviews covering performance against service levels, issues, roadmap and cost. Use scorecards to compare vendors objectively and share them with the vendors themselves.

## Managing supplier risk
Identify single points of failure and concentration risk. Mitigation options include qualifying second sources, holding safety stock, monitoring suppliers' financial health and including contractual business continuity requirements. Review the risk register at least annually and after major disruptions.
