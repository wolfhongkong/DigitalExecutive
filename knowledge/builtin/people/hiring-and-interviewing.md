# Hiring and interviewing

## Start with a scorecard
Before sourcing, define the role's mission, the three to five outcomes expected in the first year and the competencies required to achieve them. A scorecard aligns the hiring team and makes evaluation consistent. Avoid job descriptions that list tasks and years of experience without describing success.

## Sourcing
Combine referrals, direct outreach, job postings and specialist recruiters. Referrals often produce strong candidates but can reduce diversity if they are the only channel. Track source-of-hire and conversion rates to invest in the channels that work.

## Structured interviews
Structured interviews, where every candidate is asked the same job-relevant questions and evaluated against defined criteria, predict performance better than unstructured conversations. Assign each interviewer specific competencies to assess. Use behavioral questions about past situations and work samples or case exercises that resemble the real job.

## Reducing bias
Write independent feedback before discussing candidates, use rating anchors that describe what weak, good and excellent answers look like, and ensure diverse interview panels. Focus on evidence rather than impressions such as confidence or similarity to the interviewer.

## Making the decision
Hold a debrief where interviewers share evidence against the scorecard. Check references with targeted questions about the outcomes and competencies in the scorecard, including people the candidate did not nominate where appropriate and lawful. Hire for clear strengths aligned to the role rather than for the absence of weaknesses.

## Offer and onboarding
Move quickly once a decision is made; strong candidates have alternatives. Explain the full offer, including equity and benefits. A structured onboarding plan with a first-30-60-90-day plan, a buddy and early feedback reduces early attrition and shortens time to productivity.

## Legal considerations
Interview questions and background checks are regulated differently across countries. Avoid questions about protected characteristics and consult HR or legal counsel for jurisdiction-specific rules.
