# Performance management and feedback

## Purpose
Performance management should help people do their best work, not only produce ratings. It combines clear expectations, regular feedback, development and fair decisions about pay, promotion and exits.

## Setting expectations
Employees should know their role's outcomes and how success is measured. Goals can be individual or team-based and should connect to company priorities. Revisit goals when priorities change rather than evaluating people against outdated targets.

## Continuous feedback
Frequent, specific feedback is more effective than an annual review. A simple model describes the situation, the observed behavior and its impact, then asks for the other person's view and agrees on next steps. Give positive feedback as specifically as corrective feedback. Regular one-to-one meetings are the main vehicle.

## Reviews and calibration
Periodic reviews, often twice a year, summarize performance against expectations. Calibration sessions, where managers compare assessments across teams, reduce rating inflation and individual bias. Use rating scales with clear definitions and base assessments on documented evidence.

## Managing an underperforming employee
Address underperformance early and directly. Clarify the gap, explore causes such as unclear expectations, skills, motivation or personal circumstances, and agree on support and a timeline. A formal performance improvement plan should be specific, measurable and genuinely achievable. Document conversations, follow internal policy and local employment law, and involve HR before any dismissal.

## Development and retention
Discuss career goals at least annually and create development plans that combine on-the-job stretch assignments, mentoring and training. High performers leave when they lack growth, recognition or a good manager; regular conversations about engagement reduce unexpected resignations.
