# Product prioritization frameworks

## Why prioritization is hard
Product teams always face a backlog with more good ideas than capacity. To prioritize is to choose explicitly what not to do. Frameworks make trade-offs transparent and comparable, but they support judgment rather than replacing it.

## RICE scoring
RICE scores an initiative by Reach (how many users or customers it affects in a period), Impact (how much it moves the target outcome for each, on a simple scale), Confidence (how certain the estimates are, as a percentage) and Effort (person-weeks or months). The score is reach times impact times confidence, divided by effort. It is useful for comparing many medium-sized items.

## Cost of delay
Cost of delay estimates how much value is lost for each period an item is not delivered. Dividing cost of delay by duration, sometimes called weighted shortest job first, prioritizes items that deliver the most value per unit of time. It highlights time-sensitive opportunities such as regulatory deadlines or seasonal demand.

## Kano model
The Kano model classifies features as basic expectations (their absence causes dissatisfaction), performance features (more is better) and delighters (unexpected features that create enthusiasm). Basic expectations must be met first; delighters become basic expectations over time.

## Opportunity scoring
Survey customers on how important each desired outcome is and how satisfied they are with current solutions. Outcomes with high importance and low satisfaction represent the largest opportunities.

## Practical guidance
Prioritize against a clear product strategy and measurable goals, otherwise scores reflect opinions. Balance investment categories, for example new capabilities, improvements, technical health and customer commitments. Revisit priorities regularly and communicate the reasoning, including what was not chosen.
