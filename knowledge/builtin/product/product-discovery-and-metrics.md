# Product discovery and product metrics

## Discovery before delivery
Product discovery reduces the risk of building the wrong thing. It tests four risks before significant investment: value (will customers want it), usability (can they use it), feasibility (can it be built) and viability (does it work for the business, including legal, financial and brand constraints).

## Continuous customer contact
Strong product teams talk with customers every week rather than only during big research projects. Interviews should explore past behavior and real problems rather than asking customers to design solutions or predict future behavior. Map opportunities from interviews and connect them to desired outcomes.

## Experiments and prototypes
Test ideas with the cheapest experiment that can answer the key question: clickable prototypes, landing pages, concierge or manual tests, fake-door tests and limited beta releases. Define success criteria before running the experiment to avoid rationalizing results.

## Product-market fit signals
Signals include strong retention cohorts that flatten rather than decline to zero, organic growth and referrals, customers who would be very disappointed without the product, and sales cycles that shorten as the value becomes obvious.

## Choosing metrics
A north star metric captures the core value customers receive, such as weekly active collaborating teams or orders delivered. Support it with input metrics the team can influence. The AARRR funnel tracks acquisition, activation, retention, referral and revenue. Retention by cohort is usually the most important indicator of lasting value.

## Avoiding metric pitfalls
Avoid vanity metrics such as total sign-ups that only grow. Pair metrics to prevent gaming, for example growth with quality or engagement with satisfaction. Analyze by segment and cohort, and check statistical significance before acting on A/B test results.
