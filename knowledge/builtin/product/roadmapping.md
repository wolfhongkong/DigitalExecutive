# Product roadmapping

## Purpose of a roadmap
A product roadmap communicates the direction of a product and the problems the team plans to address over time. It aligns teams and stakeholders. It is a statement of intent and strategy, not a binding promise of features and dates.

## Outcome-based roadmaps
Organize the roadmap around goals and customer problems rather than a list of features. A common format uses time horizons such as Now, Next and Later, with increasing uncertainty further out. Each item states the problem, target customers and the metric it should move.

## Inputs to the roadmap
Inputs include company strategy and objectives, customer research and feedback, usage data, sales and support insights, competitive developments, technical constraints and technical debt. Weigh requests by their fit with strategy and impact, not by the seniority or volume of the requester.

## Communicating to different audiences
Executives need the link to strategy and business outcomes. Sales and customers need direction and themes, with careful language about timing. Engineering needs enough detail to plan capacity and architecture. Maintain one source of truth and produce views for each audience.

## Commitments and dates
Commit to dates only where necessary, such as contractual or regulatory deadlines, and only after discovery has reduced uncertainty. Track commitments separately and protect capacity for them. Frequent missed commitments erode trust more than honest uncertainty.

## Keeping the roadmap current
Review the roadmap regularly, for example monthly or quarterly, and update it when learning changes priorities. Explain changes and the reasons behind them so stakeholders understand that adapting is part of the process.
