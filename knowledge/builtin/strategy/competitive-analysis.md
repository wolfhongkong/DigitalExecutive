# Competitive analysis and sources of advantage

## Industry structure
The profitability of an industry depends on its structure. A widely used lens examines five forces: rivalry among existing competitors, the threat of new entrants, the threat of substitutes, the bargaining power of buyers and the bargaining power of suppliers. Strong forces compress margins; weak forces allow attractive returns. The analysis helps decide where to compete and which forces to shape, for example by raising switching costs or diversifying suppliers.

## Mapping competitors
Identify direct competitors (same customer need, similar solution), indirect competitors (same need, different solution) and potential entrants (adjacent players with relevant assets). For each, compare target customers, value proposition, pricing, distribution, capabilities, financial strength and likely next moves. Customer interviews and win-loss analysis are more reliable than marketing material.

## Sources of competitive advantage
Sustainable advantage usually comes from a small number of sources: cost advantage through scale, process or location; differentiation customers will pay for; network effects; switching costs; proprietary technology or data; brand; regulatory licenses; and exclusive access to resources or channels. An advantage is durable only if competitors find it hard to imitate or substitute.

## Positioning choices
Companies typically win either by delivering similar value at lower cost or by delivering differentiated value at a premium, within a broad or a focused segment. Trying to be everything to everyone tends to produce an undifferentiated position. Strategy involves explicit trade-offs: deciding what not to do.

## SWOT and its limits
A SWOT analysis (strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats) is useful for structuring discussion but is often too generic. Make it actionable by linking each item to evidence and to a strategic implication.

## Keeping the analysis current
Competitive landscapes shift. Assign ownership for monitoring competitors, review the analysis at least annually and whenever a major market event occurs, such as a new entrant, a price war or a regulatory change.
