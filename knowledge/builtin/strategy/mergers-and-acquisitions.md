# Mergers and acquisitions fundamentals

## Why companies acquire
Typical motives are acquiring capabilities or technology, entering new markets or segments, gaining scale and cost synergies, acquiring talent, and removing a competitor. A clear strategic rationale should exist before a target is identified; opportunistic deals without a thesis frequently destroy value.

## The deal process
A typical process includes defining the acquisition strategy and criteria, sourcing and screening targets, initial approach and non-binding indication of interest, due diligence, valuation and negotiation, signing a definitive agreement, regulatory and other approvals, closing, and integration. Each stage should have go or no-go criteria.

## Due diligence
Diligence tests the investment thesis and identifies risks. It typically covers financial quality of earnings, commercial position and customer concentration, technology and intellectual property, legal and regulatory exposure, tax, human resources and key-person dependency, and cultural fit. Findings should feed into the price, deal structure, protections in the agreement and the integration plan.

## Valuation and synergies
Common valuation methods are discounted cash flow, trading multiples of comparable companies and precedent transaction multiples. Synergies should be estimated conservatively, split into cost and revenue synergies, and assigned owners and timelines. Revenue synergies are usually harder to realize than cost synergies. Paying away all expected synergies in the purchase price leaves no margin for error.

## Integration
Most value is created or lost during integration. Appoint an integration leader, define the target operating model early, retain key people, communicate frequently with employees and customers, and track synergy realization. Decide deliberately which parts of the acquired company to integrate fully and which to keep independent.

## Common reasons deals fail
Overpaying due to competitive bidding or optimism, weak strategic fit, underestimating cultural differences, losing key customers or employees, and integration that is slow or poorly managed.
