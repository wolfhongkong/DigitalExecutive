# Strategic planning and OKRs

## From strategy to execution
A strategy states where the company will compete and how it will win. Planning translates that into a small number of priorities, resource allocation and measurable goals. The most common failure is not a poor strategy but poor translation: too many priorities, unclear ownership and goals that do not reflect the strategy.

## A practical planning process
Start with a clear diagnosis of the current situation and the key challenge. Define a guiding policy: the overall approach to the challenge. Then select coherent actions that reinforce each other. Set three to five company priorities for the year, allocate budget and people to them explicitly, and stop or defer work that does not fit.

## Objectives and key results
OKRs pair a qualitative objective with two to five measurable key results. The objective describes what you want to achieve and why it matters; key results describe how you will know you achieved it. Key results should measure outcomes, such as retention or revenue, rather than activities, such as launching a feature.

## Writing good OKRs
Keep the number small: a team with more than three to five objectives per quarter is usually unfocused. Make key results specific, time-bound and verifiable. Distinguish committed goals, which must be met, from aspirational goals, where reaching about 70 percent is considered success. Align OKRs vertically with company priorities and horizontally with dependent teams.

## Reviewing progress
Check progress regularly, for example weekly within teams and monthly at leadership level, using a simple confidence rating. Score OKRs at the end of the period and discuss lessons, not just scores. Avoid tying OKR scores directly to compensation, which encourages sandbagging.

## Scenario planning
For uncertain environments, develop several plausible scenarios based on the most important uncertainties, identify early indicators for each and prepare contingent actions. Scenario planning makes a strategy more robust without pretending to predict the future.
